01 · CONGRATULATIONS

The job is nearly yours!

So you've secured an interview. That's awesome! Whatever the outcome, take a moment to celebrate this milestone in your job hunt.

Like most things in life, practice makes perfect, and job interviews are no different. Pre-interview nerves can be overwhelming, especially if the interview is for your dream job. Head out with every intention of winning, wowing and proving to the recruiter or hiring manager that this job should be yours, but first take a moment to celebrate the fact that you secured an interview.

20%On average, only around 20% of job applicants are called in for an interview. You're one of them.

Over the next few sections we'll share tips to guide you through the interview process and set you up to succeed.

02 · PRE-INTERVIEW PREP

Do your homework.

Use your time before the interview to research the business, its leadership team, its competitors and what it offers its customers.

The goal is a good overall understanding of the business and where it sits in the market. You might want to visit as a customer and consider your impression from the customer's side.

While it's important to have this knowledge, we don't recommend repeating all your findings and opinions in the first interview. If you're asked what you know about the company, being able to say you've done a little research and visited as a customer leaves a positive impression.

Where to start

  • Visit their stores and shop there as a customer!
  • Look through the company website, including its careers page.
  • Check out the LinkedIn profiles of key people in the business.
  • View job boards to see what other positions are being advertised and how the business describes itself.
  • Ask around about the business and get a feel for how it's seen in the industry.
TIPS
  • Keep it professional. Hold off on connection or friend requests to your interviewer for now, and don't apply for other roles the business has listed.
  • Make sure you know where you're going for your interview and how you'll get there on the day. Do a practice run and know where you'll park.
KEYUnderstand the business beyond the basic facts.
03 · JAZZ UP FOR YOUR INTERVIEW

It's all about the detail.

Remember the numbers: only around 20% of applicants secure a job interview, and you're one of the lucky ones. You need to be ready to shine on the day. This might sound like common sense, but as experienced recruiters we can tell you it's an area many people overlook. Being exceptional comes down to getting the finer details right before the interview. So let's start with you.

Personal presentation

  • Your hair needs to be tidy. If it's long, consider wearing it up. How's your regrowth? Does your facial hair need a trim?
  • Make sure your nails are clean and neat, with no chipped polish.
  • If you wear glasses, give them a good clean.

What to wear?

  • Match your style to the brand. For a role in a streetwear store you don't need a suit; for a role in a corporate support office, leave the streetwear at home.
  • Have your interview clothes clean, ironed and ready ahead of time. There's nothing worse than finding a stain on your shirt as you head out the door.
  • Coming straight from work? Your uniform is fine as long as you've told the interviewer and checked first. Just make sure it's clean!
  • Give your shoes a clean and polish. If they're scuffed or damaged, consider a new pair.
  • Less is best with jewellery. Consider removing facial piercings for the interview and find out later what the workplace allows.
  • Some workplaces don't allow visible tattoos. Keep them covered for the interview and check the company's policy later.
TIPS
  • Feel comfortable and like yourself in what you're wearing.
  • Don't force the fit. If a business's dress code, jewellery or tattoo policies aren't for you, the business may not be either.
  • Your presentation is part of your personal brand and leaves a lasting impression, so make it a positive one!
GOLDEN RULE

It's always better to be slightly overdressed than underdressed.

04 · INTERVIEW DAY

It's show time!

The big day is finally here! An interview is a fact-finding process for both the interviewer and you as the candidate. At the first interview, your job is to make the interviewer love you. There'll be time later to explore the business further and ask detailed questions. The first interview is not that time!

Enjoy the interview and be yourself. Your resume got you this interview and the interviewer wants you to be great, so be ready to talk about your resume and experience in detail.

Getting started

  • Arrive on time: no more than five minutes early, and not even a minute late.
  • You may need to complete paperwork or application forms, so bring ID such as a passport or driver licence, and fill in every detail accurately.

TIPS
  • Practise your handshake with friends and family.
  • You'll most likely be greeted at reception. Greet the person politely and let them know who you're there to meet.
  • Every contact you have with the business creates an impression, and your first interaction with the front-line team is often passed on to the interviewer. We've seen this go both ways. We shouldn't have to say it, but be nice to everybody!
04 · SHOW TIME CONTINUED

Know your answers.

At the start of the interview there's usually some friendly small talk. Use this time to put yourself at ease, as the interviewer will likely move on quickly to your resume and past experience.

Be genuine, be yourself, be honest. If you're asked a question and you're unsure of the answer, admit you don't know and don't dwell on it.

Know your resume inside out. Be ready to talk in depth about specific experiences, especially situations that relate to the role you've applied for. Prepare for behavioural and situational questions: structure your answers and use real examples, so you answer the actual question.

Behavioural questions

Based on the idea that past behaviour predicts future behaviour, these ask how you did act in a real situation, not how you would. They often start with "Describe a time when…" or "Give me an example of…"

Situational questions

These focus on hypothetical situations rather than your past experience. They can be skill specific or draw out your interpersonal style, and often start with "How would you…" or "What would you do if…"

Other questions

Sometimes the trickiest questions are the simplest. Candidates often struggle with questions about themselves, such as:

  • If I asked your team to describe you, what would they say?
  • Why should I employ you over other candidates?
  • Why do you want to work here?

Use the STAR technique.

04 · SHOW TIME CONTINUED

Finish strong.

Let the interviewer control and guide the interview. A good interviewer will always give you the chance to talk at some point.

Interviewers want to see your interest in the role and its scope, the team and the company in general. Towards the end you might be asked if you have any questions, so it's a good idea to have a few ready.

1–3questions in a notebook is plenty at a first interview. It shows you've thought about the role and the business and you're taking the career move seriously. Make sure they're genuine, haven't already been answered and suit a first meeting.
  • Don't bring up money or pay rates unless you're asked about your current salary or expectations. If you are asked, be honest: salary checks are becoming more common before an offer is made.
  • Be ready for all sorts of questions and remember, you're selling and the product is you! You know yourself better than anyone, so give it your all.

At the end of the interview, thank them for their time and ask what the next step will be if they haven't already told you.

TIPS
  • Turn your mobile phone off. Don't even leave it on silent.
  • Don't leave your sunglasses on your head.
  • Place your bag on the floor beside you.
  • No chewing gum.
  • Don't fidget. Think calm, and rest your hands on your lap or the table.
KEYLet the interviewer guide your interview. You will get the chance to talk.
05 · THE WAITING GAME

Patience pays off.

Your interview doesn't end when you leave the room. How you behave afterwards is just as important as the interview itself.

Relax

Be kind to yourself and try not to overthink how it went. Don't dwell on the parts you feel didn't go so well. If you were given a business card, it's nice to send a very short thank-you email, for example:

"Thank you for your time. It was great to meet you and I'm really enthusiastic about the business and the opportunity. I look forward to hearing from you and hope I'm successful in progressing."
If you're working with a recruiter, copy them in.

Be patient

Wait for them to contact you within the time frame they gave. If you haven't heard back by then, contact the interviewer to ask how their search is going. Be open to feedback and don't pressure them for an answer. In the meantime, keep busy and throw yourself into your current role!

TIPS
  • Stay active as a job seeker until you have a new role secured. Make it your job to find a job: search daily for new opportunities and stay in touch with your recruiter and networks.

06 · CHIN UP

Handle it like a superstar.

You can't win them all, and sometimes it's just not meant to be. Handle rejection well: thank the interviewer for their time and the opportunity. If it's appropriate, ask them to keep your details on record for other suitable vacancies.

The first choice may not accept the offer, or could be counter-offered by their current employer, so you want to be the best second choice in town. How you handle rejection can make or break a second chance with the business in the future, and this happens more often than people realise.

It would be great to get constructive feedback, but if it's not offered, don't push. If the interviewer has honest, valuable feedback, they'll offer it. There's a fine line between learning from the experience and being demanding.

Now is a good time to connect with the people you met on professional networks such as LinkedIn. If the company is an employer of choice for you, follow its activity in the market and keep an eye on its hiring opportunities.

KEYHow you handle rejection can make or break your chance of a second opportunity.
